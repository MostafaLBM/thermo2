PROCESS CONTEXT

Process scenario

Presets are illustrative end states, not equipment ratings. Mixing of two streams is not a single-chord process and is not included.

Model assumptions & properties

Moist air is an ideal-gas mixture of dry air and water vapor at the standard-atmosphere pressure for the chosen altitude, p = 101.325 (1 − 2.25577×10⁻⁵ Z)5.2559 kPa. Saturation pressure over water (t ≥ 0 °C) or ice follows Hyland and Wexler as given in ASHRAE Handbook—Fundamentals (2017), ch. 1. The enhancement factor is taken as 1, which lowers the saturation humidity ratio by about 0.4 % relative to the published ASHRAE tables (14.70 vs 14.76 g/kg at 20 °C). Enthalpy h = 1.006 t + W (2501 + 1.86 t), referenced to dry air and liquid water at 0 °C. Wet bulb is the thermodynamic wet bulb from the ASHRAE adiabatic-saturation relations.

The chart window is that of ASHRAE Chart No. 1: dry bulb 0–50 °C, W ≤ 30 g/kg. The process is drawn as the chord between the two end states. Condensate is assumed to leave at the outlet temperature for the coil load; liquid or steam enthalpy added during humidification is not included in the air-side loads. The apparatus dew point extends the chord to saturation and assumes a straight coil process.

Duct mode: each coil uses the apparatus-dew-point model, state out = state in − (1 − BF)(state in − saturated state at the coil surface temperature). Condensate is assumed to leave at the coil surface temperature. Each humidifier is adiabatic with the injected water carried at the stated temperature; injection is limited so the outlet stays unsaturated and on the chart. Steam is treated as an ideal vapor, h = 2501 + 1.86 t.

Tower mode: Merkel counterflow model with Lewis factor 1; water loss is neglected in the water energy balance, supersaturation (fog inside the tower) is not modeled, and ambient temperature gradients with height and wind are ignored. Hot-water inlet is limited to 30–50 °C so the exit air fits the extended chart window (W up to 70 g/kg).
