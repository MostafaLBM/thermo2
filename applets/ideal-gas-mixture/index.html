DALTON’S LAW

Ideal gases add. Nonideal gases can differ.

Measure A alone and B alone at the same temperature and in the same box. Add those two pressures, then compare with the pressure when both gases share the box.

Ideal gases: Pmixture = PA alone + PB alone

Nonideal gases: Pmixture ≠ PA alone + PB alone in general

For ideal gases, each gas exerts the same pressure whether it is alone or mixed with another gas, so their individual pressures add.

For nonideal gases, mixing changes how the particles interact. They occupy space and exert forces on one another, so each gas no longer behaves as it did alone. The separately measured pressures therefore don’t generally add up to the mixture pressure.

In this demo, the nonideal particles have finite size. Adding the other gas crowds the box and introduces collisions between the two species, raising the mixture pressure above the sum measured in the separate boxes.

Choose Ideal gases: the bars match. Choose Nonideal gases: finite-size interactions make the mixture pressure higher than the sum of the individual pressures in this example. Allow the readings to settle.

Model, limits & further reading

Hard disks of equal diameter σ in a 2D box with elastic walls; σ = 0 gives the non-interacting ideal gas. Each system is initialized with kinetic energy corresponding to the same selected temperature. Elastic collisions conserve that energy; changing an input restarts all pressure averages. Pressure is the time-averaged momentum transfer to the walls per unit time and wall length. η = Nπσ²/(4A) is the area fraction. Units are reduced (kB = 1), with masses 1 and 4. Collisions are elastic and mass-weighted. Readings need a couple of seconds to settle after any change.

Reading: Çengel, Boles & Kanoglu, Thermodynamics: An Engineering Approach, 9th ed., Chapter 13.
